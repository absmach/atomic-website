Before you start

Custom domains are included on paid plans: Builder connects one app to a custom domain, and Pro up to ten. Free apps are published on their Atomic address. You need a published app, the owner or an admin role on it, and a domain you manage at a DNS provider. Registration is separate from Atomic.

Atomic connects subdomains, such as booking.example.com or www.example.com, one per app. A root domain such as example.com, wildcard names and automatic DNS changes are not supported.

Connect a domain

  1. Open the app’s Settings and find Custom domain.
  2. Enter the subdomain and choose Connect domain.
  3. At your DNS provider, add the two records Atomic shows: a CNAME record pointing the subdomain to Atomic’s connection address, and a TXT record that proves you own it. Some providers add your root domain to record names automatically. Keep the TXT record until the domain is connected.
  4. Atomic checks the records, requests an HTTPS certificate and verifies secure access on its own. When it is done, the panel shows Ownership verified, DNS connected and HTTPS active, and the app’s links use your domain.

Keep the records DNS-only. Proxying the domain through a service such as Cloudflare is not supported.

Connection status and renewal

The panel shows each step, what to do next and when Atomic will check again. DNS changes can take a while to become visible: Atomic retries automatically, and you can ask for a check. HTTPS certificates renew automatically. The connection history stays visible, including earlier problems. Editors can read the history; the DNS records and controls are for the owner and admins.

Your Atomic address, and removing a domain

Your app’s Atomic address keeps working after you connect a domain. Remove domain stops serving the app at your domain at once; the Atomic address stays available. The name can be connected again once Atomic has finished cleaning up. Deleting an app removes its custom domain too. See publish your app for the publishing workflow.
